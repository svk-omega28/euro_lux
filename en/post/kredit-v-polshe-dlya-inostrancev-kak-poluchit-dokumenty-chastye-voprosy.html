Loan In Poland For Foreigners: How To Get, Documents, Frequently Asked Questions

Obtaining a loan in Poland for foreigners is now available without the need to have Polish citizenship. This applies to both EU citizens and foreigners from other countries. The loan application process includes compliance with certain conditions, including the presentation of documents confirming legal residence and financial stability. Documentation requirements may vary depending on the borrower's country of origin.



Loan In Poland For Foreigners: Conditions For Foreigners


Obtaining a loan in Poland for foreigners is becoming an increasingly important topic in light of globalization and increased migration. This blog post aims to highlight the key points and requirements that foreigners need to consider when seeking financial support in Poland.


Our goal is to provide a comprehensive overview of the process, starting from the main criteria and ending with the necessary package of documents. We will also look at the specific loan programs available to foreigners and discuss how these conditions may differ from standard offers for Polish citizens.


This post will be useful both for those who are just starting their journey in obtaining a loan in Poland, and for those who have already encountered some obstacles and are looking for additional information. We hope that this guide will become a reliable assistant in navigating financial matters in Poland for any foreigner.


Basic Requirements For Obtaining A Loan


To obtain a loan in Poland for foreigners, it is important to take into account a number of key requirements and conditions. First of all, you need to pay attention to the following aspects:



  1. Credit History: Foreign borrowers must provide evidence of a credit history. This may include information about previous loans, debts and their timely repayment. Having a positive credit history increases your chances of getting a loan approved.

  2. Creditworthiness: This is an assessment of the borrower's ability to repay the loan. Banks may require evidence of stable income, such as salary certificates, employment contracts or other financial documents confirming the ability to repay loan funds on a timely basis.

  3. Legal Residence: Foreign borrowers must confirm their legal residence in Poland. This may be a temporary or permanent visa, residence card or other documents confirming the legality of residence in the country.

  4. Documentation: It is important to provide all necessary documents, including a passport, documents confirming identity and residential address, as well as any additional paperwork required by a particular bank.
    These basic requirements are standard for most banks in Poland, however specific conditions may vary depending on the credit institution.


Documents Required For Applying For A Loan


To apply for a loan in Poland, foreigners require a number of documents:



  1. Passport: The main document proving the identity of the applicant. Must be valid at the time of application and for the entire loan period.

  2. Visa or Residence Card: It is necessary to confirm the legality of stay in the country. Depending on your residence status, this may be a work visa, a temporary or permanent residence card.

  3. PESEL: Polish national identification number, which is issued to foreigners living in Poland. It is necessary for maintaining tax and social accounting.

  4. Certificate of Salary/Employment Contract: Documents confirming a stable income. An employment contract for an indefinite period is usually preferred as it indicates the stability of the applicant's financial situation.

  5. Credit History: Documents confirming previous loan obligations and their fulfillment. Reports from credit bureaus or copies of previous loan agreements may be required.

  6. Proof of Legal Residence: This may include a certificate of registration of residence in Poland.

  7. Additional Documents: Depending on the bank, additional documents may be required, such as certificates of financial status, proof of residential address or other papers.


These documents provide the bank with the necessary information to assess the foreigner’s creditworthiness and decide whether to grant a loan. It is important that all documents are current and correctly completed.


Loan Programs For Foreigners: Review Of Available Loan Programs Especially For Foreigners


In 2024, various lending programs are available to foreigners in Poland, among which the “Safe Loan 2%” program stands out. This program provides subsidies from the state budget for the purchase or construction of the first property. The key conditions of the program are as follows:



  • The program is aimed at people under 45 years of age who have never owned a home in Poland.

  • The state will pay banks extra for mortgage loans for 10 years so that the interest rate for the borrower is 2%.

  • The maximum loan amount is 500 thousand zlotys for one person and 600 thousand zlotys for families.

  • The maximum contribution of a program participant is 200 thousand zlotys.

  • Housing purchased under the program must be used exclusively for residence and not for investment purposes.
    In addition, certain conditions and restrictions apply. For example, if you decide to sell or rent out your home within the first 10 years of receiving your loan, this may result in the subsidies being cancelled.


It is also important to note that mortgages are available to foreigners, but not all banks provide such services. Among those offering mortgages to foreigners are Alior Bank, BPS bank, BGŻ BNP Paribas, Credit Agricole, ING Bank Śląski, Millennium Bank and PKO BP. Obtaining a mortgage loan requires a temporary or permanent residence card, as well as proof of residence and regular income in Poland.


Loan Application Process: Loan Application Process Steps


The process of applying for a loan in Poland for foreigners includes several key steps:



  1. Credit Assessment: Before applying for a loan, it is important to ensure that you have a stable source of income and a positive credit history. Banks can evaluate your banking history and check your solvency, which includes reviewing your employment contract, tax returns and income statements.

  2. Preparation of necessary documents: The standard set of documents for obtaining a loan includes a foreign passport, permission to stay in Poland (for example, a residence card), and an individual national identification number (PESEL). Depending on the type of loan and the requirements of the bank, additional documents may be required, such as an employment contract or bank account statement.

  3. Selecting a bank and submitting an application: You need to choose a bank that offers loan programs for foreigners and suits your needs. There are a number of banks in Poland offering loans to foreigners, including Alior Bank, BPS bank, BGŻ BNP Paribas, Credit Agricole, ING Bank Śląski, Millenium bank, Pekao and PKO BP. The application can be submitted online or directly at a bank branch.

  4. Waiting for the bank's decision: After submitting the application, the bank will analyze the information provided and make a decision on issuing a loan. If the bank refuses a loan, you can try to apply at another bank, since the loan terms and requirements may differ.

  5. Pay attention to the terms of the loan: Before signing a contract, it is important to carefully review the terms, especially the interest rates and any additional fees or commissions. Pay attention to the RRSO (real annual percentage rate), which takes into account all the associated costs of the loan.
    These steps are general guidelines and may vary depending on the specific bank and loan type. If necessary, it is recommended to seek advice from a bank specialist or financial adviser.



Frequently Asked Questions


Foreigners who want to get a loan in Poland face a number of questions. What do foreigners most often ask about loans in Poland? Below you can find answers to the most pressing questions regarding credit.



  1. What is needed to get a loan in Poland?
    Banks in Poland can lend up to 95% of the cost of housing, while the borrower usually pays 15-20% of the cost of the property themselves. When considering a loan application, the bank takes into account the applicant’s credit history, banking history, solvency, and also requires permission from the Polish Ministry of Internal Affairs when purchasing real estate with a land plot.

  2. Do you need a Life Card to get a loan?
    To obtain a loan, you must have legal residence in Poland and a stable source of income. Indefinite-term contracts are usually preferred, but in recent years banks have become more flexible to other types of employment contracts.

  3. Can I get a loan in Poland if I don’t have a Life Card?
    Obtaining a classic loan without a residence permit is difficult, but alternative options are possible, such as payday loans.

  4. Will I be denied a loan if I have a bad credit history?
    Every bank is required to check the client's creditworthiness. This takes into account education, marital status, place of work, level of earnings, age and credit history.

  5. Is it possible to use microloans?
    Personal loans or "microloans" should be avoided as they usually offer very high interest rates and can lead to greater financial difficulties.

  6. Can I get a loan if I don’t speak Polish?
    If you do not have a good command of the Polish language, it is recommended that you visit the bank with someone who speaks Polish. It is important to read all contracts carefully and understand the terms of the loan before signing.

  7. Which bank to choose to get a loan in Poland?
    There are a number of banks in Poland that offer loans to foreigners, including Alior Bank, BPS bank, BGŻ BNP Paribas, Credit Agricole, ING Bank Śląski, Millennium bank and others. Each bank may have its own additional requirements for foreigners applying for a mortgage.


Conclusion


In Poland, foreigners face certain requirements and conditions when applying for loans, but at the same time they have real opportunities for financial support. Having a stable source of income, legal residence, a positive credit history and meeting bank criteria increase the chances of loan approval.


Although the process may be more time-consuming compared to locals, the availability of a variety of banking programs allows expats to find suitable financing options, from mortgages to short-term loans. Overall, credit opportunities for foreigners in Poland represent a useful tool for realizing personal and professional goals in the country.
