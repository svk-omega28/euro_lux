Minimum and Average Salary in Poland in 2024

2024 was a significant year for work in Poland, bringing changes that impacted wage levels in the country. In this blog post, you will learn news about the minimum and average wages in Poland, forecasts and analysis of salary trends to assess the economic health of the country and plan your career.



We'll look at average salary statistics by occupation and region, compare them to previous years, and discuss trends that may impact the future. Particular attention is paid to various sectors of the economy where income levels can vary significantly, which is important for those looking for work in Poland.


What is the minimum salary in Poland 2024


2024 has become a year of new economic challenges and opportunities for Poland. The country has demonstrated significant resilience and ability to adapt to changing global economic conditions, which has had a noticeable impact on its labor market and wage levels. World economic events play an important role, including trade wars, changes in international economic agreements and global crises, which directly affect the Polish economy.
On September 14, the Polish Council of Ministers adopted a resolution on the minimum wage for 2024. From January 1, it will increase to PLN 4,242 before taxes, and the minimum hourly rate will be set at PLN 27.70. Moreover, from July 1, the minimum wage will be increased to 4,300 zlotys (about 930 euros), and the minimum hourly rate will be 28.10 zlotys (about 6.1 euros).
A thorough understanding of salary trends not only contributes to the analysis of the current economic state of Poland, but also allows us to predict future changes in the labor market.


How to get more or what affects the average salary?


The average salary in Poland is determined by several key factors that shape the country's financial landscape. This is especially true when considering the average salary in Poland in 2023 in dollars.


Economy


Poland's economic stability and growth could encourage higher wages. High demand for goods and services contributes to the growth of enterprises, which leads to higher wage levels for employees, especially in the context of the minimum wage in Poland.


Demand for professions


Sectors with increased demand for labor tend to offer higher salaries. For example, IT specialists and engineers often receive higher remunerations, which is especially important when considering working in Poland for Ukrainians.


Education and experience


The level of education and professional experience of an employee influence the amount of his salary. Highly qualified specialists with higher education and significant experience often occupy higher paying positions.


Regional differences


Salary levels may vary in different regions. For example, in large cities such as Warsaw, salaries are usually higher than in smaller localities, which is a significant aspect when considering the minimum salary in Poland.


Inflation and economic changes


Factors such as inflation and changes in economic policy affect purchasing power and, therefore, the real income of workers.
A thorough understanding of these factors helps workers make smart decisions about their careers and financial planning, choosing career paths that align with current job market trends.


Statistics on average salary


What is the average salary in Poland? In 2023, the average salary in Poland varied depending on the region. In the Masovian Voivodeship it reached 5,860 zlotys, while in the Pomeranian and Lower Silesian Voivodeships the average salary was approximately 5,100 zlotys. The lowest average wage was recorded in the Lubuskie and Świętokrzyskie voivodeships, where it fluctuated around 4200-4300 zlotys.


Average salary in Poland 2024: forecasts by profession


Salaries in various sectors of the Polish economy in 2023 show interesting trends, reflecting the demand and importance of various professions in the country. In the IT sector, which is one of the most dynamically developing, the average salary reaches PLN 8,000, which indicates the high value of specialists in this field, especially in the context of ongoing digitalization and technological progress.



  1. IT specialists:

    • Software development specialists: from PLN 12,000 to PLN 20,000 per month.

    • Information security engineers: from 15,000 to 25,000 zlotys per month.



  2. Medical workers:

    • Specialist doctors: from 15,000 to 25,000 zlotys per month.

    • Nurses: from 8,000 to 12,000 zlotys per month.



  3. Financial sector:

    • Financial analysts: from 10,000 to 18,000 zlotys per month.

    • Accountants: from 8,000 to 15,000 zlotys per month.



  4. Production and logistics:

    • Production engineers: from 10,000 to 15,000 zlotys per month.

    • Logistics specialists: from 8,000 to 14,000 zlotys per month.



  5. Customer Service and Retail:

    • Customer service managers: from 6,000 to 10,000 zlotys per month.

    • Retail salespeople: from 4,000 to 8,000 zlotys per month.



  6. Warehouse workers:

    • Warehouse worker: from 5,000 to 8,000 zlotys per month.

    • Logistics specialist in a warehouse: from 6,000 to 10,000 zlotys per month.



  7. Workers in production:

    • Production worker: from 4,000 to 7,000 zlotys per month.

    • Production line operator: from 5,000 to 8,000 zlotys per month.




In 2024, Poland will see a noticeable increase in wages even in industries that require less skilled workers, such as cleaners and electricians. In these areas, previously considered less paid, there is high potential with salaries starting from PLN 7,000 gross. This indicates a significant increase in economic opportunities and rising labor costs in various sectors.

Expert opinions and forecasts for the next years


For 2024, experts predict that global economic trends will have a significant impact on the labor market and wage levels in Poland. Increased integration into the global economy and continued technological development are expected to increase demand for high-skilled occupations, which in turn could lead to higher average wages.
This also has an impact on immigration and the labor market. High wages attract workers from abroad, especially from low-income countries, leading to changes in the structure of the labor force. The Polish labor market includes workers from various nationalities, among which a significant number are Ukrainians, Belarusians, Kazakhs and others who are looking for higher wages and better working conditions.
Such changes require flexibility and adaptation both from the Polish labor market and from immigrants seeking to integrate into the new economic environment.
